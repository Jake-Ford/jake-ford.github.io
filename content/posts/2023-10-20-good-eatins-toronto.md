Toronto has an embarrassment of culinary riches, which makes it simultaneously exciting and overwhelming to navigate. After 18+ months here, I've developed some strong opinions. This is a living document — I'll update it as I discover more.

The original post had an interactive Leaflet map with all the spots pinned. In the meantime, here are the categories and highlights:

## Breakfast / Brunch

Toronto's brunch scene is serious business. A few spots I keep returning to:

- The egg sandwich situation at various Portuguese cafés along Dundas West is a religious experience. Pastel de nata while you're there, obviously.
- Stockyards (Junction) — the smash burger at brunch hours is not a mistake.

## Lunch

- The St. Lawrence Market is worth a full afternoon: peameal bacon sandwich on a kaiser is the canonical Toronto lunch, full stop. Don't argue with me.
- Kensington Market for the chaotic, walk-around, sample-everything experience. Best done on a Saturday.

## Dinner

Toronto's dinner scene is genuinely world-class. The diversity of the city means you can eat extraordinarily well across cuisines that you'd have to travel to eat elsewhere.

- **Scarborough** for the best Chinese, South Asian, and Caribbean food in the city — honestly in North America. The food courts along Sheppard and Kennedy are not to be slept on.
- **Koreatown on Bloor** — galbi and soju on a weeknight, need I say more.
- **Little Portugal / Little Italy on College** — great for a long leisurely evening with wine.

## Bars / Drinks

- Any bar that isn't charging you $22 for a cocktail is doing God's work in this city.
- The brewery tap rooms in Leslieville and the Junction are an excellent way to spend a Saturday afternoon.

## The Underrated Truth

The best food in Toronto is not at the trendy restaurant your New York friends told you about. It's at the strip mall on a suburban arterial road where a family has been running the same regional cuisine for 20 years. Find these places. Ask your colleagues where they grew up eating. This is the move.
