Geographic Concentration of the Eras Tour Setlist Data

2025-01-23 · Where does Taylor Swift draw her inspiration from?

The Eras Tour — the highest-grossing concert tour in history — wrapped up in late 2024 after 149 shows across 5 continents. Beyond the economic spectacle, there's an interesting data question buried in the setlist: does the geographic distribution of Taylor Swift's songwriting inspirations cluster in any meaningful way?

The short answer: yes, pretty heavily in the American South and Northeast, which tracks pretty directly with Swift's biographical arc from Reading, Pennsylvania → Nashville → New York City.

The Methodology

Using the full Eras Tour setlist data (which rotated "surprise songs" throughout the tour, making this a larger dataset than typical), I mapped references — place names, specific locations, and regionally-coded lyrical themes — to geographic coordinates. The reactable package in R makes for a nicely interactive table to explore the full data.

Findings

A few things stood out:

Nashville dominates. The country-era albums (Taylor Swift, Fearless, Speak Now, Red) are heavily Tennessee-coded — references to small-town romance, pickup trucks, and the specific emotional geography of the American South.

New York is well-represented but concentrated. The 1989 era brings in a distinct New York energy (most explicitly on "Welcome to New York" and "New Romantics"), but even beyond the obvious tracks, there's a shift in lyrical texture.

The surprise songs added chaos in the best way. With Swift debuting deep cuts and vault tracks nightly, the surprise song data actually pulls in the widest geographic spread — more international references, older and more obscure Americana.

The full interactive table with song-by-song breakdowns is worth exploring if you're at all into this kind of thing. The patterns in what she wrote about, when, and where those places are, tells a pretty coherent coming-of-age story mapped onto American geography.
